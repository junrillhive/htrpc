Calm & protect your rhythm

Atrial fibrillation (AFib) is an irregular, often rapid electrical rhythm in the heart’s upper chambers (atria). Because blood can pool when the atria quiver instead of contracting smoothly, untreated AFib increases stroke risk fivefold.

AFib rhythm & stroke care

Up to 30% of people with atrial fibrillation never feel racing palpitations·noticing only unexplained fatigue or shortness of breath on stairs.

AFib Rhythm Care

Heart Rhythm Guide · Reviewed by Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC

Appropriate anticoagulation in eligible AFib patients reduces cardioembolic stroke risk by nearly 65% to 70%.

Spotting Irregular Rhythms

Whether your irregular pulse was caught during a routine physical at Suite 622, flagged by an Apple Watch or Fitbit ECG notification, or diagnosed in the hospital, long-term primary care keeps you safe and active.

Classic & Subtle AFib Symptoms

Report a “flip-flopping” or racing chest sensation, sudden drop in walking stamina, lightheadedness, mild chest pressure, or smartwatch irregular-rhythm alerts promptly.

Diagnostic Workup & Root Causes

We evaluate resting ECGs, wearable ambulatory patch monitors, thyroid function (TSH/Free T4, as hyperthyroidism triggers AFib), serum potassium and magnesium, and echocardiogram coordination.

CHA₂DS₂-VASc Stroke Protection

We calculate your personalized stroke score based on age, blood pressure, diabetes, and vascular history, managing modern Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) with periodic kidney checks.

Taming Everyday AFib Triggers

Controlling high blood pressure, treating obstructive sleep apnea, staying hydrated, limiting alcohol (“holiday heart”), and avoiding stimulant cold pills markedly reduce AFib episodes.

Frequently asked questions

Evidence-based answers from our Portsmouth primary care clinicians regarding atrial fibrillation (afib), lab testing, and follow-up care.

My Apple Watch or Fitbit alerted me to possible Atrial Fibrillation. What should I do?

If you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or feel faint, call 911. If you feel comfortable, save or print the PDF rhythm strip from your phone app and call our Portsmouth office at (757) 516-7554 for a same-week evaluation, clinical pulse check, thyroid/electrolyte panel, and ambulatory rhythm monitor.

If I feel fine and my heart rate is normal, do I still need a blood thinner for AFib?

Yes, if your CHA₂DS₂-VASc stroke risk score indicates it. Even when AFib is intermittent (paroxysmal) or asymptomatic, silent episodes can still allow clots to form in the upper chamber of the heart. Never pause your anticoagulant (such as Eliquis or Xarelto) without consulting your clinician.

Does drinking coffee or alcohol trigger atrial fibrillation?

Alcohol is a well-documented direct trigger for AFib episodes (“holiday heart syndrome”), and cutting back or eliminating alcohol significantly reduces recurrence. While 1 cup of morning coffee is safe for many patients, high-dose energy drinks, excess caffeine, and over-the-counter decongestants can provoke palpitations.

How does sleep apnea affect atrial fibrillation?

Untreated obstructive sleep apnea causes drops in nighttime oxygen that trigger surges of adrenaline and pressure inside the heart’s atria·making AFib much harder to control even with medication. Treating sleep apnea often cuts AFib episodes in half.
