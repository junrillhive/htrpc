Daily hypertension care

Sustainable blood pressure control rests on four pillars: hidden-sodium awareness, potassium-rich foods, restorative sleep, and well-matched medication.

Hypertension Plan

Daily Care Plan · Reviewed by Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC

Over 70% of dietary sodium comes from packaged breads, canned soups, deli meats, and restaurant sauces rather than your salt shaker.

Food-First & Smart Medication

Whether you are managing borderline Stage 1 readings with nutrition or simplifying a multi-pill regimen, each step in our Portsmouth care plan has a measurable effect on your systolic number.

Cutting Hidden Sodium (5–8 mmHg Drop)

Aim for 1,500 to 2,000 mg of sodium daily. Check nutrition labels for anything over 300 mg per serving, rinse canned beans and vegetables, and season fish and poultry with garlic, lemon, smoked paprika, and herbs.

Potassium-Rich Foods (4–5 mmHg Drop)

Unless restricted by chronic kidney disease, dietary potassium helps your kidneys flush excess sodium and relaxes arterial walls. Build meals around leafy greens, sweet potatoes, avocados, lentils, and berries.

Tailored First-Line Medications

We match ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, or thiazide-like diuretics to your age, kidney labs, and ethnicity, using single-pill combinations whenever possible to reduce pill burden.

Sleep Apnea & Morning Spikes

Untreated obstructive sleep apnea triggers nighttime adrenaline surges that cause stubborn morning hypertension. If you snore or wake unrefreshed, we coordinate home sleep apnea testing.

Frequently asked questions

Evidence-based answers from our Portsmouth primary care clinicians regarding hypertension care plan, lab testing, and follow-up care.

All 8 Clinical Guides
What should I do if my blood pressure pill causes a dry cough or swollen ankles?

Never stop your medication abruptly without letting us know, as rebound blood pressure spikes can occur. A dry cough is a classic harmless side effect of ACE inhibitors (like lisinopril), and mild ankle swelling can happen with calcium channel blockers (like amlodipine). Call (757) 516-7554 and we can switch you to an ARB or alternative class that eliminates the side effect.

Are cold and sinus decongestants safe to take when I have hypertension?

Oral decongestants containing pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine constrict blood vessels and can dangerously spike blood pressure and heart rate. Daily NSAID pain relievers (like ibuprofen or naproxen) also raise blood pressure and strain kidneys. Ask our team about safe nasal saline sprays, antihistamines, or acetaminophen alternatives.

Should I take my blood pressure medication in the morning or at bedtime?

The most effective time is the time of day you will remember consistently every single day. However, diuretics (“water pills” like chlorthalidone or hydrochlorothiazide) are best taken in the morning so they do not interrupt your sleep.

Why do I need a blood test a few weeks after starting a new blood pressure medication?

Medications like ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and diuretics protect your kidneys over the long term by changing pressure inside renal filters and shifting sodium and potassium levels. A quick Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP) 2 to 4 weeks after starting ensures your potassium and creatinine stay in a safe range.
