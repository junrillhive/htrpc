Know your blood pressure

High blood pressure is known as the “silent killer” because readings of 140/90 mmHg or higher rarely cause headaches, nosebleeds, or dizziness until significant artery and kidney damage has already occurred. Accurate home monitoring between visits is the fastest path to protection.

Blood Pressure Guide

Cardiovascular Guide · Reviewed by Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC

Every 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure lowers major cardiovascular events by 20% and stroke risk by 27%.

Accurate Home Cuff Protocol

Before starting or increasing blood pressure medication at 355 Crawford Street, Suite 622, we ask patients to record a 7-to-14-day home log using a validated automatic upper-arm cuff rather than a wrist or finger monitor.

5-Minute Rest Preparation

Empty your bladder first, avoid caffeine, exercise, or tobacco for 30 minutes, sit with your back supported and feet flat on the floor (uncrossed), and rest your bare upper arm at heart level.

Two Readings, Morning & Night

Take two readings one minute apart each morning before taking medications and each evening before dinner. Bring your written log or cuff memory to Suite 622 so we can average the true trend.

Protective Kidney & Heart Labs

Unchecked pressure strains delicate renal filters. We order an annual Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP), eGFR kidney check, serum potassium, lipid panel, and urine microalbumin ratio.

Hypertensive Crisis Warning

If your cuff reads 180/120 mmHg or higher without symptoms, rest 5 minutes and retest, then call (757) 516-7554. If paired with chest pain, shortness of breath, numbness, or vision loss, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

All 8 Clinical Guides
Why is my blood pressure higher at the doctor’s office than at home?

This is known as “white-coat hypertension.” Commuting traffic, clinic nervousness, or talking during a measurement can raise systolic readings by 10 to 25 mmHg. Bringing a 14-day log from a validated upper-arm home cuff lets us treat your true resting baseline rather than a temporary spike.

Can I feel when my blood pressure is elevated?

Almost never. Most adults with readings in the 140s, 150s, or even 160s feel completely normal. Waiting for headaches, flushing, or dizziness before checking your cuff allows years of preventable wear on your heart and kidney vessels.

Which home blood pressure monitor do your clinicians recommend?

We recommend an automated, validated upper-arm cuff (such as Omron or Welch Allyn home models listed on ValidateBP.org) sized properly to your upper arm circumference. Bring your cuff to your appointment at 355 Crawford Street, Suite 622 and we will check its accuracy against our clinical monitor.

If I start blood pressure medication, will I have to take it forever?

Not always. For patients with Stage 2 hypertension, medication provides immediate vascular protection while you build sustainable nutrition, sodium, sleep, and movement habits. As weight, insulin sensitivity, and sodium balance improve, we frequently step down dosages safely.
