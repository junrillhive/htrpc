Master your 90-day A1C

Unlike a single fasting fingerstick that fluctuates from hour to hour, Hemoglobin A1C measures the percentage of red blood cell proteins coated with glucose over the past 90 days. Knowing your A1C gives you a clear, judgment-free roadmap.

Prediabetes & A1C Control

At 355 Crawford Street, Suite 622, we build diabetes care around your real kitchen, work schedule, and insurance coverage·combining food-based glucose stabilization with organ-protective therapies.

Quarterly A1C & Glucose Targets

For most adults with Type 2 diabetes, we aim for an A1C below 7.0%, pre-meal fasting glucose between 80 and 130 mg/dL, and 2-hour post-meal glucose under 180 mg/dL without low-blood-sugar episodes.

The Diabetes Plate Method

No complicated calorie counting: fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables (collards, broccoli, salad, green beans), one-quarter with lean protein (fish, chicken, eggs, tofu), and one-quarter with high-fiber carbs.

Heart & Kidney Protective Meds

Alongside foundational metformin, we prescribe modern SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists when indicated to lower A1C, support weight loss, and protect kidney and heart function.

Annual Foot, Kidney & Eye Shield

Every diabetes care plan at Suite 622 includes an annual monofilament nerve foot exam, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR), eGFR kidney panel, and dilated retinal eye exam coordination.

Frequently asked questions

Evidence-based answers from our Portsmouth primary care clinicians regarding type 2 diabetes & a1c guide, lab testing, and follow-up care.

All 8 Clinical Guides
Do I have to fast before a Hemoglobin A1C blood test?

No. Because Hemoglobin A1C measures glucose attached to red blood cells over a 90-day lifespan, eating breakfast before your appointment will not change your A1C percentage. However, if we are also checking a fasting lipid panel or fasting metabolic panel on the same morning, we recommend an 8-to-12-hour water-only fast.

Can prediabetes really be reversed without lifelong medication?

Yes. Clinical studies show that when patients with an A1C between 5.7% and 6.4% lose 5% to 7% of their body weight, walk 150 minutes per week, and replace sugary drinks and refined starches with high-fiber whole foods, progression to Type 2 diabetes drops by 58%.

What are the signs of low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) I should watch for?

A blood glucose reading below 70 mg/dL causes shakiness, cold sweats, sudden hunger, rapid heartbeat, irritability, or dizziness. Follow the “Rule of 15”: consume 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate (4 oz of juice or 3–4 glucose tablets), wait 15 minutes, and recheck your blood sugar.

Can you prescribe Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs) at Hampton Roads Primary Care?

Yes. We prescribe and interpret wearable continuous glucose sensors (such as Dexcom and FreeStyle Libre) for eligible patients so you can see real-time glucose curves on your smartphone without frequent fingersticks.
