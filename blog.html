Your First Primary Visit

Featured Clinical Article · What to Expect at Your First Visit With a Primary Care Provider

Whether you recently moved to Hampton Roads, transitioned out of the military or TRICARE system, or simply haven’t seen a doctor in several years, scheduling your first primary care appointment is the single most impactful step you can take for your long-term health.

Unlike urgent care clinics that focus on a single 10-minute symptom, your first visit at Hampton Roads Primary Care establishes a complete baseline of your cardiovascular, metabolic, and emotional health · so you have a trusted clinical team who knows your name and medical history before an illness ever arises.

What to Bring to Suite 622

Arriving 10 to 15 minutes early (or completing your digital intake forms in the OptiMantra Patient Portal before you leave home) allows us to spend the full appointment focused on your health goals. Please bring:

Photo ID & Insurance Card: Bring a valid driver’s license, state ID, or military ID along with your Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, Anthem, or Sentara card. If you are applying for our $25–$65 Sliding-Scale Fee Program, bring your prior year Form 1040, SSA Award Letter, or SNAP Notice of Action.

Actual Medication & Supplement Bottles: Rather than relying on memory, place your current prescription bottles, over-the-counter pain relievers, inhalers, and herbal supplements in a bag so your clinician can verify exact milligram dosages and check for drug interactions.

Home Blood Pressure or Glucose Logs: If you check your blood pressure or blood sugar at home, bring your 14-day written log or bring your cuff into Suite 622 so we can calibrate it against our clinical monitor.

Step-by-Step Through Your Appointment

Vital Signs & Baseline Screenings: Our clinical team records your resting blood pressure (after 5 minutes seated), heart rate, oxygen saturation, height, weight, and BMI, plus a brief depression and anxiety wellness screen.

Unhurried Medical & Family History Conversation: Your provider · Aaron Sents, PA-C, Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC, or Shanice Edwards, NP-BC · reviews your past surgeries, hospitalizations, allergies, sleep quality, nutrition, and immediate family history of heart disease, stroke, diabetes, or cancer.

Comprehensive Physical Examination: We perform a thorough head-to-toe exam including heart and lung auscultation, carotid and peripheral pulse checks, abdominal exam, neurological reflexes, and skin or foot evaluation as indicated.

Personalized 90-Day Care Roadmap: Before you leave, we agree on a clear plan: which preventive lab panels (CBC, CMP, lipid panel, A1C, TSH, Vitamin D) or age-appropriate cancer screenings to order, which prescriptions to refill or simplify, and when to follow up in person or by Virginia telehealth.

BP Cuff Tips

Heart & Vascular · By Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC

Small cuff positioning errors can artificially inflate your home blood pressure reading by 10 to 40 mmHg.

How to Get Clinic-Grade Accuracy from Your Home Monitor

Many patients arrive worried about a spike on their home monitor that turns out to be a cuff-fit or posture error. Avoid these five everyday pitfalls when logging your numbers:

Placing the Cuff Over Clothing

Rolling up a tight sleeve creates a tourniquet effect, and placing the cuff over fabric adds 5 to 50 mmHg. Always slide your arm out of the sleeve completely.

Crossing Legs or Dangling Feet

Sitting on the edge of a bed with feet dangling or crossing your legs at the knee raises systolic pressure by 2 to 8 mmHg. Sit in a dining chair with feet flat on the floor.

Measuring With a Full Bladder

A full bladder triggers sympathetic nerve activity that can add 10 to 15 mmHg to your reading. Always use the restroom before sitting down for your 5-minute rest.

Using a Wrist Cuff Below Heart Level

Wrist monitors are highly sensitive to arm angle. We strongly recommend an upper-arm validated cuff rested on a tabletop at mid-chest level.

Sick Visit Guide

Acute Care & Triage · By Aaron Sents, PA-C

With Emergency Medicine and Navy IDC experience, Aaron Sents, PA-C explains when a same-week primary care visit saves you time, money, and repeat visits.

Why Seeing Your Own Primary Care Team Matters When You’re Sick

When a sinus infection, persistent cough, urinary tract infection, or ankle sprain strikes, many people automatically drive to a walk-in urgent care box · paying a higher specialist copay to see a provider who has no access to their kidney function, allergy list, or baseline medications.

When to Call Suite 622 First

Fever, sore throat, sinus or ear pain, bronchitis, asthma flares, UTIs, rashes, minor cuts/sprains, or sudden blood pressure spikes without chest pain. Call (757) 516-7554 at 8:00 AM.

Safer Prescribing With Your Chart

Certain antibiotics and decongestants can dangerously raise blood pressure or interact with blood thinners and diabetes medications. Your primary care team checks your full chart first.

Lower Primary Care Copays

Most Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, and commercial plans charge a significantly lower copay for a primary care sick visit than for urgent care or freestanding emergency rooms.

When to Go Straight to the ER (Call 911)

Crushing chest pain, severe shortness of breath, sudden stroke signs (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech), major trauma, or uncontrolled bleeding always require 911 / Emergency Department care.

Food-Based A1C

Metabolic Nutrition · By Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC

You don’t need expensive specialty groceries to lower your blood sugar · just strategic pairing of fiber, protein, and timing.

Three Everyday Plate Shifts That Flatten Post-Meal Glucose Spikes

Hemoglobin A1C reflects both your fasting morning blood sugar and the glucose spikes that happen 60 to 120 minutes after meals. In our Portsmouth nutrition consultations, we focus on sustainable habits using foods from your neighborhood grocery store:

Eat Fiber & Protein First

Starting your meal with leafy greens, non-starchy vegetables, and protein (fish, chicken, eggs, beans) slows gastric emptying and reduces the glucose spike from carbohydrates eaten afterward by up to 30%.

Eliminate Liquid Sugars

Sweet tea, regular soda, fruit juice, and sweetened coffee creamers hit the bloodstream within 10 minutes because they contain zero fiber. Swapping to unsweetened tea or infused water often drops A1C by 0.5% to 1.0% on its own.

The 10-Minute Post-Dinner Walk

Walking at a comfortable pace for just 10 to 15 minutes after your largest meal activates skeletal muscle glucose uptake without requiring extra insulin.

Pair Lifestyle With Lab Tracking

Because red blood cells turn over every 90 days, we recheck your A1C and metabolic panel every 3 months so you can see the exact proof of your effort.

Mental Wellness

Whole-Person Care · By Shanice Edwards, NP-BC

Chronic stress, insomnia, and anxiety directly impact blood pressure, immune resilience, and blood sugar control.

Why Your Primary Care Office Is a Safe First Stop for Emotional Wellness

Many adults in Hampton Roads wait months for a specialty psychiatry appointment when they could begin compassionate, confidential treatment right in their primary care office this week.

Rule Out Medical Mimics First

Thyroid imbalances (hypo- or hyperthyroidism), Vitamin D or B12 deficiency, iron-deficiency anemia, and perimenopause frequently masquerade as fatigue, brain fog, or panic.

Trauma-Informed, Judgment-Free Care

We listen without rushing, use validated PHQ-9 and GAD-7 tools to track progress, and coordinate both non-controlled medication support and behavioral therapy referrals.
