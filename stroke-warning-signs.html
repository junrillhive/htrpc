Spot stroke signs fast

During an ischemic stroke, nearly 1.9 million brain cells are lost every single minute blood flow is blocked. Recognizing the B.E. F.A.S.T. warning signs and calling 911 immediately unlocks clot-busting treatment within the golden window.

Stroke Recognition

Emergency & Vascular Guide · Reviewed by Aaron Sents, PA-C

Up to 80% of first and recurrent strokes are preventable through blood pressure, lipid, diabetes, and AFib control.

Recognizing Stroke Minutes Early

Whether symptoms are dramatic or vanish after 10 minutes (a Transient Ischemic Attack or “mini-stroke”), every sudden neurological change is a medical emergency.

B.E. · Balance & Eyes

Balance: Sudden loss of coordination, unsteadiness, stumbling, or severe room-spinning dizziness. Eyes: Sudden blurred vision, double vision, or loss of sight in one or both eyes.

F.A.S.T. · Face, Arm, Speech, Time

Face: One side droops or feels numb when smiling. Arm: One arm drifts downward or feels weak/numb. Speech: Slurred words or trouble understanding. Time: Call 911 and write down the exact clock time.

Never Ignore a TIA (“Mini-Stroke”)

If facial droop, arm weakness, or slurred speech resolves in 5 to 15 minutes, it is likely a Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)·a warning clot that carries a high risk of a major stroke within 48 hours.

Primary & Post-Stroke Prevention

At Suite 622, we target the top modifiable stroke drivers: keeping blood pressure under 120–130/80 mmHg, LDL cholesterol under 70 mg/dL, A1C under 7.0%, treating AFib, and quitting tobacco.

Frequently asked questions

Evidence-based answers from our Portsmouth primary care clinicians regarding stroke warning signs & care, lab testing, and follow-up care.

All 8 Clinical Guides
Why is it so important to note the exact minute stroke symptoms started?

Clot-dissolving medications (such as intravenous alteplase or tenecteplase) must be given within 3 to 4.5 hours of when the person was “last known well,” and mechanical clot removal has strict time windows. Knowing the exact clock time helps emergency neurologists deliver brain-saving therapy safely.

Should I give someone an aspirin if I think they are having a stroke?

No. Never give aspirin, food, water, or any medication during a suspected stroke. Roughly 13% of strokes are hemorrhagic (caused by a bleeding vessel in the brain), where aspirin can worsen bleeding·and strokes frequently impair swallowing, creating a choking hazard. Wait for a hospital CT scan first.

What if the slurred speech or arm numbness goes away after 10 minutes?

Still call 911 or go to the nearest stroke-certified emergency department immediately. Temporary stroke symptoms indicate a Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA). Even though the clot broke up temporarily, the risk of a full, permanent stroke is highest in the first 48 hours after a TIA.

How does primary care at Suite 622 help after a hospital discharge for TIA or stroke?

Post-hospital transitional primary care is critical to prevent a second stroke. We reconcile your hospital discharge medications (antiplatelets, high-intensity statins, blood pressure agents), monitor kidney and liver labs, coordinate physical/speech therapy, and screen for post-stroke fatigue or depression.
