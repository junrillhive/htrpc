Cardiac arrest vs. heart attack

Plumbing vs. electrical

Think of a heart attack as a “plumbing problem” (a blocked artery starves heart muscle of oxygen while the person remains awake) and sudden cardiac arrest as an “electrical problem” (the heart’s rhythm stops pumping abruptly and the person collapses unconscious).

Two Distinct Cardiac Emergencies

Knowing whether someone is experiencing a heart attack or sudden cardiac arrest tells you immediately how to help in the first 60 seconds while 911 EMS is on the way.

Heart Attack (“Plumbing Blockage”)

A clot blocks a coronary artery supplying oxygen to the heart muscle. The person is awake and breathing. Symptoms include center-chest pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain radiating to the jaw, neck, back, or left arm.

Heart Attack Signs in Women & Seniors

Women, older adults, and patients with diabetes frequently experience atypical heart attack symptoms: sudden shortness of breath, cold sweats, unexplained nausea/indigestion, dizziness, or extreme upper-back fatigue.

Cardiac Arrest (“Electrical Failure”)

A lethal electrical arrhythmia stops the heart from pumping blood to the brain. Within seconds, the person collapses, does not respond when tapped on the shoulder, and stops normal breathing (or only gasps).

Hands-Only CPR & AED Action Plan

For cardiac arrest: 1) Call 911 on speakerphone, 2) Push hard and fast in the center of the chest (100–120 beats/min), and 3) Have someone bring an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) and follow its voice prompts.

Do I need to do mouth-to-mouth breaths during CPR for an adult who collapses?

No. For teens and adults who suddenly collapse, the American Heart Association recommends Hands-Only CPR: call 911 and push hard and fast in the center of the chest at 100 to 120 beats per minute (the beat of the song “Stayin’ Alive”) without stopping until EMS arrives or an AED is ready.

Can anyone use a public Automated External Defibrillator (AED) without training?

Yes. Public AEDs (found in airports, schools, gyms, shipyards, and office buildings) are designed for bystanders with zero medical training. As soon as you open the lid or press the power button, a calm voice tells you where to place the two sticky pads, analyzes the heart rhythm automatically, and will only allow a shock if a shockable cardiac arrest rhythm is present.

What non-emergency symptoms should prompt a same-week primary care heart check?

If you notice new chest tightness, pressure, or burning when walking uphill or carrying groceries that goes away when you rest (exertional angina), unexplained drop in stamina, fluttering palpitations, or a family history of early heart attack, call (757) 516-7554 for a prompt clinic evaluation.

Why should I call 911 instead of having a family member drive me during chest pain?

Because a heart attack can trigger sudden cardiac arrest in transit. EMS paramedics carry a 12-lead ECG machine, oxygen, emergency medications, and a defibrillator right into your living room, and they pre-activate the hospital cardiac catheterization team before you even arrive.
