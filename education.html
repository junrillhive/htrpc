Blood pressure and heart guides

Reviewed by Laurie A. Salerno, ANP-BC · drawing on 34 years in cardiology and internal medicine · to help you control hypertension and protect your arteries.

Blood Pressure

Cardiovascular Guide · Reviewed by Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC

Often called the “silent killer,” high blood pressure rarely causes headaches or dizziness until significant artery damage has occurred.

How to Take an Accurate Home Reading

White-coat tension in a medical office can temporarily raise systolic pressure by 10 to 20 mmHg. For the most accurate clinical picture, we ask Portsmouth patients to use a validated upper-arm automatic cuff (rather than a wrist or finger monitor) twice daily for 7 to 14 days before their appointment.

5-Minute Rest Protocol

Empty your bladder, avoid caffeine or nicotine for 30 minutes, sit with your back supported and feet flat (uncrossed), and rest your bare arm at heart level on a table.

Understanding the Stages

Normal is below 120/80 mmHg. Elevated is 120–129/<80. Stage 1 Hypertension is 130–139 or 80–89. Stage 2 Hypertension is 140/90 mmHg or higher.

Hypertensive Urgency Signs

If your reading exceeds 180/120 mmHg without symptoms, rest 5 minutes and retest; call our clinic at (757) 516-7554. If paired with chest pain, shortness of breath, or vision loss, call 911.

Protective Kidney & Heart Labs

We check a Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP), eGFR kidney function, serum potassium, and urine microalbumin annually to ensure your vessels stay protected.

Hypertension Care

Daily Care Plan · Reviewed by Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC

Lowering systolic pressure by just 10 mmHg reduces the risk of major cardiovascular events by 20% and stroke by nearly 27%.

Combining Food-Based Nutrition With Tailored Medication

At Hampton Roads Primary Care, we never hand out a prescription without a practical daily roadmap. Whether you are starting lifestyle therapy for Stage 1 hypertension or simplifying a multi-pill regimen, our goal is steady 24-hour blood pressure control without fatigue or dizziness.

Sodium Under 1,500–2,000 mg

Over 70% of dietary sodium comes from packaged breads, canned soups, deli meats, and restaurant sauces rather than the salt shaker. Rinse canned beans and season with garlic, lemon, and herbs.

Potassium-Rich Whole Foods

Unless restricted by kidney disease, potassium-rich foods · leafy greens, sweet potatoes, avocados, beans, and berries · help your kidneys excrete excess sodium and relax vessel walls.

Smart Medication Pairing

We tailor ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, or thiazide diuretics to your age, kidney function, and lab profile, using single-pill combinations whenever possible.

Sleep Apnea & Stress Checks

Untreated obstructive sleep apnea is one of the most common causes of resistant morning hypertension. We screen for daytime fatigue and snoring during your visit.

Heart Prevention

Preventive Cardiology · Reviewed by Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC

Atherosclerosis develops silently over decades. Early lipid profiling and inflammation checks stop plaque progression before chest pain begins.

Beyond Total Cholesterol: Understanding Your Cardiac Risk

Standard cholesterol numbers tell only half the story. In our Portsmouth clinic, we evaluate your complete lipid panel · LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, and non-HDL cholesterol · alongside blood pressure, A1C, family history, and 10-year ASCVD risk score.

LDL & Non-HDL Targets

For primary prevention, we aim for LDL below 100 mg/dL; for patients with diabetes or prior vascular disease, we target LDL below 70 mg/dL (or <55 mg/dL in very high risk).

Triglycerides & Metabolic Health

Fasting triglycerides above 150 mg/dL often signal insulin resistance. Cutting sugary drinks and refined starches lowers triglycerides faster than low-fat diets alone.

150 Minutes of Brisk Movement

Thirty minutes of brisk walking five days a week along the Portsmouth seawall or neighborhood trails strengthens cardiac output and improves insulin sensitivity.

Statin & Non-Statin Options

If medication is indicated, we discuss the exact risk reduction and monitor liver enzymes and muscle comfort · using ezetimibe or low-dose regimens if statin sensitivity occurs.

Practical protocols for Type 2 diabetes, atrial fibrillation, stroke warning signs, cardiac arrest recognition, and tobacco cessation.

Type 2 Diabetes

Metabolic Guide · Reviewed by Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC

Hemoglobin A1C measures your average blood glucose over the past 90 days · giving us a reliable roadmap to protect your eyes, kidneys, and nerves.

Prediabetes Reversal & Type 2 Diabetes Control

More than one in three adults in Hampton Roads has prediabetes (A1C 5.7% to 6.4%), often without knowing it. Catching elevated blood sugar during this window allows food-based nutrition, modest weight loss (5% to 7% of body weight), and targeted therapy to prevent progression to Type 2 diabetes.

Quarterly A1C & Glucose Goals

For most adults with Type 2 diabetes, we target an A1C below 7.0%, fasting morning blood glucose between 80 and 130 mg/dL, and post-meal readings below 180 mg/dL.

Plate Method Nutrition

Fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables (greens, broccoli, peppers), one-quarter with lean protein (fish, poultry, beans), and one-quarter with high-fiber complex carbohydrates.

Modern Cardiorenal Medications

Beyond metformin, we utilize GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors when appropriate to lower A1C while protecting kidney function and heart health.

Annual Foot, Eye & Kidney Checks

Every diabetes care plan at Suite 622 includes a monofilament foot exam, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and coordination for your annual dilated retinal exam.

AFib Rhythm Care

Heart Rhythm Guide · Reviewed by Laurie Salerno, ANP-BC

AFib is an irregular, often rapid heart rhythm originating in the upper chambers of the heart that increases stroke risk fivefold if left untreated.

Spotting Irregular Heart Rhythms Early

Some patients feel atrial fibrillation immediately as a racing or “flip-flopping” sensation in the chest, while others notice only unexplained fatigue, shortness of breath when climbing stairs, or lightheadedness.

Symptoms to Report Promptly

Fluttering palpitations, sudden drop in exercise stamina, dizziness, mild chest pressure, or smartwatch irregular-pulse alerts warrant a prompt primary care evaluation.

Rate & Rhythm Management

We coordinate resting ECGs, wearable ambulatory rhythm patches, thyroid and electrolyte labs, and rate-control medications to keep your resting heart rate comfortable.

CHA₂DS₂-VASc Stroke Protection

We calculate your individualized stroke risk score and manage modern direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) safely with periodic kidney and blood count monitoring.

Reducing AFib Triggers

Controlling blood pressure, treating sleep apnea, limiting alcohol (“holiday heart”), and avoiding stimulant decongestants significantly reduce AFib episodes.

Stroke & Arrest

Emergency Recognition · Reviewed by Aaron Sents, PA-C

Knowing the difference between a stroke, a heart attack, and sudden cardiac arrest saves lives across Hampton Roads every day.

B.E. F.A.S.T. Stroke Signs & Heart Attack vs. Cardiac Arrest

During a stroke, nearly two million brain cells are lost every minute blood flow is blocked. Memorize the B.E. F.A.S.T. acronym and always call 911 immediately rather than driving yourself to the hospital.

B.E. · Balance & Eyes

Balance: Sudden loss of coordination, stumbling, or severe dizziness. Eyes: Sudden blurred, double, or lost vision in one or both eyes.

F.A.S.T. · Face, Arm, Speech, Time

Face drooping on one side; Arm weakness or numbness; Speech slurred or confused; Time to call 911 and note the exact minute symptoms started.

Heart Attack (“Plumbing Issue”)

A blocked coronary artery starves heart muscle of oxygen. The person is awake and breathing, with chest pressure, jaw/back/arm pain, cold sweats, or nausea. Call 911 immediately.

Cardiac Arrest (“Electrical Issue”)

An electrical malfunction stops the heart from pumping abruptly. The person collapses, is unresponsive, and stops normal breathing. Call 911, start hands-only CPR, and use an AED.

Quit Smoking

Preventive Counseling · Reviewed by Shanice Edwards, NP-BC

Quitting tobacco with clinician guidance and medication support more than doubles your success rate compared to quitting cold turkey.

What Happens to Your Body After Your Last Cigarette

Whether you have smoked cigarettes, cigars, or vaped nicotine for two years or forty years, your cardiovascular and respiratory systems begin repairing themselves within minutes of quitting.

20 Minutes to 72 Hours

Within 20 minutes, heart rate and blood pressure drop. Within 12 hours, carbon monoxide levels normalize. By 72 hours, bronchial tubes relax and breathing becomes easier.

1 Month to 1 Year

Lung cilia recover to clear mucus and reduce cough within 1 to 9 months. At 1 year, your excess risk of coronary heart disease drops to half that of a current smoker.

Medical Quit Aids

We prescribe and coordinate insurance coverage for dual nicotine replacement (24-hour patch plus rapid gum/lozenge), varenicline, or bupropion tailored to your health history.

Low-Dose CT Lung Screening

For current or former smokers aged 50 to 80 with a 20+ pack-year history, we order annual low-dose CT lung screenings to detect lung nodules at their earliest, curable stage.
